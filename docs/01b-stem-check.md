# LevelHull STEM check

Verdict: the working principle of LevelHull (closed-cell foam fixed high inside the hull cuts the waterline of the swamped canoe along both sides, so it floats upright on its own foam) sits within known science, and so do the principles of options B and C. Of the 34 claims, **27 are within known science, 5 are plausible and need field data or a test at TRL 4 and later** (S10 cover and webbing life, S17 sustained bailing rate, S23 decay at screw holes, S27 abrasion of option B's sponsons, S33 roll behaviour of a sealed hull brim-full), and the 2 rows that failed with a fix proposed to Amish (S32, S34) are now within known science: straight after swamping a sealed canoe is brim-full and sits about 24 mm up (about 3 mm at its lowest point), so the 50 mm of R2 and the bail-out time of R4 held only once the water inside was down to the lake level. Amish decided on 2026-10-25 ("i concur with your recommendations") to keep the hull sealed and state R2 and R3 for the free-flooding condition of the USCG method with the operating rule "hold on, then bail at once, seated" (LVH-DEC-001 D-O2), and to supply four bailers so all four crew bail, about 11 min from brim-full (D-O3). Of the 34 claims, 29 are now within known science and 5 plausible. `stem_check` is **open**. Two details were fixed under Amish's standing instruction of 2026-10-23: the end chock screws went only 10 mm into the batten (S13, D-S1), and galvanised coach screws are withdrawn in favour of A4 stainless (S22, D-S2). The independent review also corrected the calculation note's floor comparison and its handling of timber denser than water. Method: STANDARDS section 20. Calculations: `docs/04-calcs/stem_check.py`, which reads LVH-CAL-001 (`docs/04-calcs/sizing.py`, `results.csv`) and the model, and writes `stem_results.csv` and `options_results.csv`.

> **Safety:** LevelHull is flotation for a boat, not lifesaving equipment for people, and it is never a substitute for life jackets. These checks are screening calculations for a paper design; no fitted canoe is used before its fixings are pull-tested and it has passed a swamp test in sheltered, shallow water with a safety boat and every person in a life jacket.

## Results added by this check

- **Straight after swamping (S2, S32, S33).** The calculation note, like the USCG level flotation method (33 CFR 183.220(g): "allowing water to flow between the inside and outside of the boat"), treats the water inside as part of the lake. A planked canoe has no opening below the gunwale, so after a wave fills it to the brim the water inside stays above the lake level and rises with the hull. The reserve at zero freeboard (277 kg) over the outer waterplane (about 11.4 m2) then holds the gunwale about 24 mm up, about 3 mm at the low end. In that state the canoe is near neutral in roll (S33), so it lolls a degree or two until a gunwale dips and water spills out, which lowers the inside level; bailing does the rest, and at the lake level the 92 mm of LVH-CAL-001 follows. The crew should expect a canoe nearly awash at first.
- **Pessimistic inputs (S7, S31).** Design net load 20 % higher and foam lift 5 % lower together: 76 mm at the lowest point. Wet timber at 1,100 or 1,200 kg/m3 instead of 1,000: 75 and 57 mm. All above the 50 mm of R2 in the lake-level condition.
- **Partly bailed (S18).** At 200 mm freeboard with all four crew aboard and 2.3 m3 of water still inside, the free surface of that water costs about 0.28 m of metacentric height; 0.14 m remains, with the crew seated (the reviewer's centre of weight gives about 0.10 m). Crews bail sitting low, not standing.

## Fields not relevant

None. Every field is touched: physics (hydrostatics, stability, strength of fixings and booms), chemistry (foam water uptake and fuel resistance, polymer UV life, corrosion), biology (crew physiology, bailing effort, wood decay), mathematics (units, orders of magnitude, sensitivity) and engineering (fastener practice, tolerances, fitting time). Branches such as electromagnetism, optics, acoustics and nuclear radiation are not relevant: the kit has no electrical, optical or radiating parts.

## Concept options

- **Option A, foam modules high inside (the design):** S1 to S24 and S31 to S34. Within known science except the field items (S10, S17, S23, S33) ; the two sealed-hull rows S32 and S34 are within known science since Amish's decisions D-O2 and D-O3 of 2026-10-25.
- **Option B, external foam sponsons:** within known science (S25, S26): 158 mm lowest freeboard and twice A's roll stiffness, because the same foam sits lower and further out. Its 32 bolt holes through the planks are below the swamped waterline and must be bedded watertight. S27, abrasion and tearing of the external covers, is plausible, to confirm in field use. Losing one of its four sponsons sinks the gunwale 32 mm under (R12 not met as drawn); see LVH-OPT-001.
- **Option C, double outrigger floats:** within known science (S28 to S30): 160 mm lowest freeboard, very high roll stiffness, booms at 8.5 MPa against 16 MPa when a float is driven under, floats safe against collapse. Losing one float lets the canoe capsize; see LVH-OPT-001.

The brim-full condition of S2 and S32 to S34 applies to all three options: B and C also have sealed hulls. No option has a Fails row; none is dropped on science.
